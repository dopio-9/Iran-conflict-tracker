TRACE — Seismograph v2

A redesign that recommits to the instrument: spikes, bloom, and a pen that's alive at NOW.
GOALS  ① make it read as a seismograph again  ·  ② make the trace luminous  ·  ③ make the present moment feel like a live recording  ·  ④ let balance be legible at a glance.

WHAT CHANGED · AND WHY
1
Phosphor bloom on the trace
was: a flat 1.2px hairline with no glow — the least luminous element of the most luminous metaphor. Now a layered halo + bright core, like a real CRT/oscilloscope line.
2
A live pen-head at NOW, with a sweep line
was: the trace just stopped, dead, at the current time. The drama of a seismograph is the pen writing — so NOW is a pulsing point on the leading edge.
3
Event spikes restored — deflection size = kcal
was: the metaphor was abandoned for a smooth meander (the unused mag field is the fossil of this). It read like a thermocline, not a seismograph. Each event now fires a sharp spike — intake right, burn left.
4
Balance-tinted glow — mint deficit, amber surplus
was: the trace was always green, even deep in surplus, and green meant both "the recording" and "burn." The wash under the curve now warms to amber as you climb into surplus, cools to mint when you're under.
5
Leader lines tie each label to its event
was: on a busy day, staggered labels chained downward and detached from their dots — and burn labels sat far left of dots that were far right. Thin leaders re-anchor name → moment.
6
An integrated balance gauge (−500 … +500)
was: a generic four-column number strip. The needle now shows where you sit against neutral at a glance — the readouts stay underneath for the exact figures.
Also fixed quietly: SVG no longer stretches the trace (was preserveAspectRatio="none"), the zero-axis now glows as a real datum, and overnight idle-drain reads as a cool drift before the day's first spike.
